Build Instructions 🔧

Assembling an AIDrift car takes roughly one afternoon with basic tools: a small Phillips screwdriver, a soldering iron, and tweezers. Before starting, order everything on the Parts page and print the mounts (STL files in the repository).

1

Prepare the chassis

Disassemble the stock 1:43 chassis: remove the body shell, stock electronics, and the front steering assembly. Keep the drivetrain and wheels.

  • Remove the two body clips and lift off the shell
  • Unscrew the stock PCB and set it aside
  • Check that the rear axle spins freely
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Bare chassis
2

Fit the drift tires

Swap the stock rubber tires for low-grip drift rings. Consistent, low lateral grip is what makes controlled side slip possible at this scale.

  • Slide the stock tires off the rims
  • Fit the printed/PVC drift rings on all four wheels
  • Verify all four wheels have the same diameter
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Drift tires fitted
3

Install steering servo

Replace the stock bang-bang steering magnet with a micro proportional servo — continuous steering control is essential for holding a drift.

  • Glue the servo mount into the front bay
  • Connect the servo horn to the steering knuckles with the printed linkage
  • Center the servo before tightening the horn screw
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Servo installed
4

Solder the electronics

Wire the motor driver, IMU, and receiver to the controller board according to the wiring diagram. Keep leads short — space is tight at 1:43.

  • Solder motor leads to the driver outputs
  • Connect the IMU over I²C (keep the wires twisted)
  • Connect servo signal, 5 V, and GND
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Wiring complete
5

Mount controller and battery

Stack the controller board on the printed plate above the rear axle and fit the 1S LiPo as low and as central as possible — weight distribution strongly affects drift balance.

  • Screw the controller to the printed plate
  • Velcro the battery into the center bay
  • Check the center of mass sits just behind the midpoint
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Electronics stacked
6

Add the tracking marker

Attach the ArUco marker plate on top of the car. The overhead camera uses it for position and heading ground truth during data collection and evaluation.

  • Print the marker at exactly the specified size
  • Mount it flat — any tilt biases the heading estimate
📷 PHOTO PLACEHOLDER
Marker mounted
7

Flash the firmware and calibrate

Flash the firmware, calibrate the IMU and steering center, and run the first system check. See the Train page for the software pipeline.

  • Flash firmware over USB
  • Run calibrate_imu with the car stationary
  • Trim steering center until the car tracks straight
