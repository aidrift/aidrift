AIDrift: An Open-Source Platform for Autonomous Drifting at 1:43 Scale

Author One · Author Two · Author Three · Author Four

Your Lab / University, City, Country

Abstract — Drifting — driving at the limit of tire adhesion with sustained, controlled side slip — is one of the most demanding control problems in autonomous driving. Full-scale drift research requires closed proving grounds, expensive vehicles, and significant safety infrastructure, placing it out of reach for most labs and classrooms. We present AIDrift, an open-source autonomous drifting platform at 1:43 scale that brings aggressive, at-the-limit vehicle dynamics onto a tabletop. The platform combines a low-cost chassis, an onboard IMU, and overhead visual localization with a lightweight drift controller that sustains high side-slip cornering, transient drift initiation, and tandem maneuvers on tracks that fit inside a typical classroom. Every component — hardware, firmware, controller, and training pipeline — is documented and reproducible with off-the-shelf parts. By shrinking drift research to pocket scale, AIDrift aims to make the study of at-the-limit autonomy accessible to students and researchers everywhere.

Welcome to AIDrift

AIDrift is a low-cost, open-source platform for research and education in at-the-limit vehicle control. On a track the size of a desk, it initiates, sustains, and chains drifts — the same control problem studied on full-scale vehicles, at a fraction of the cost and risk.
